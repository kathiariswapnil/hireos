The problem

Enterprise hiring is coordination work, not decision work

Recruiters and hiring managers spend their week moving information between systems that do not talk to each other. The judgment takes minutes. The logistics take weeks.

01

Fragmented stack

Hiring runs across ATS, HRMS, job portals, email, calendars, Slack or Teams, spreadsheets and finance approvals. No system owns the workflow end to end.

02

Repetitive coordination

Drafting JDs, screening applicants, arranging interviews, chasing reminders, collecting feedback and preparing offers absorb the majority of recruiting hours.

03

Generic AI does not know your company

Off-the-shelf AI has no view of your approval hierarchy, salary bands, role levels, interview standards or hiring history.

04

Black-box AI is unadoptable

An AI that simply outputs hire or reject creates accountability and compliance risk no enterprise will absorb.

The judgment is the job. Everything around it is overhead an operating layer should absorb.

Our thesis

Separate the model from the mandate

Every credible enterprise hiring agent needs two brains. Most products build only the first one.

Language-heavy work

AI layer

  • Understand the hiring request
  • Generate and revise JDs
  • Summarize resumes and interviews
  • Extract competency evidence
  • Generate interview questions
  • Draft candidate communication

Recommends. Drafts. Never decides.

POLICY ENGINE

Rules, states and authority

Deterministic layer

  • Salary bands and limits
  • Approval hierarchy
  • Permissions and RBAC
  • Workflow state transitions
  • Required interview rounds
  • Audit logs and traceability

Evaluates. Blocks. Holds the authority.

The rule that defines the product

An LLM may recommend an action. The policy and workflow engine decides whether that action is allowed.

A candidate expects INR 28 LPA against an approved L3 band of INR 18–25 LPA. The model does not get to call that acceptable. The policy engine flags the exception and routes it to the approver who owns it.

Most products build the first brain and call it a hiring agent. The second brain is what an enterprise is actually buying.

Agent architecture

Specialized agents with scoped tool permissions

No agent gets unrestricted authority. Each one receives only the tools its job requires — which is what turns a demo into something an enterprise security review will pass.

Requisition Agent

MVP

Turns a natural-language request into structured requirements.

⌗Reads role knowledge · writes draft requisition

JD Agent

MVP

Generates and revises JDs from approved knowledge and templates.

⌗Writes JD draft · cannot publish

Candidate Intelligence

MVP

Extracts evidence and compares candidates to approved requirements.

⌗Reads applications · writes evaluation

Scheduling Agent

V2

Finds compatible slots, confirms and reschedules.

⌗Calendar and comms · no hiring decisions

Interview Intelligence

V3

Transcribes, summarizes and maps evidence to competencies.

⌗Transcript access · writes feedback draft

Offer Agent

V3

Prepares compensation and offer drafts within policy.

⌗Writes draft · cannot send without approval

Governance Layer

Enforces authorization, policy, auditability and human gates across every agent. No unrestricted model authority anywhere in the system.

An agent that could publish a job, message a candidate and send an offer is a security finding, not a feature.

Adoption model

Earn autonomy one rung at a time

Enterprises do not buy autonomy on day one. They grant it after the system proves itself. That progression is our expansion motion, and the override data we collect is what makes the next rung defensible.

  1. Rung 1 · Land here

    Copilot

    AI recommends. Human executes.

    Candidate recommendation with evidence.

  2. Rung 2 · Core of the product

    Assisted

    AI prepares the action. Human approves.

    JD, shortlist and offer drafts.

  3. Rung 3 · Expand here

    Autopilot

    AI executes low-risk actions within policy.

    Interview reminders, approved notifications.

No rung grants autonomy over a hiring decision. Autopilot moves reminders and approved notifications. Shortlists, feedback, selection and compensation stay gated on a recorded human decision at every rung — that is a property of the state machine, not a configuration flag.

Moving a customer from Copilot to Assisted to Autopilot raises both the value we deliver and the price we can defend.

Enterprise readiness

Built for the security review, not retrofitted for it

Enterprise HR data is among the most sensitive a company holds. The architecture assumes that from the first commit.

Resumes are untrusted input

A candidate can put instructions in a resume. Uploaded documents are never allowed to issue commands to an agent.

Read the trust architecture
  • Tenant isolation and least privilege
  • Encryption in transit and at rest
  • Configurable retention and deletion
  • Model and prompt version traceability
  • Full audit trail on every material action
  • PII classification and controlled access
  • Secrets and key management
  • SOC 2 / ISO 27001 pathway as we mature
